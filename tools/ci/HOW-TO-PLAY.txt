Riftrite - playtest build
========================================

WINDOWS
  1. Unzip, then double-click Riftrite.exe.
  2. The build isn't code-signed, so Windows SmartScreen may warn you the
     first time: click "More info", then "Run anyway".

MACOS
  1. Unzip, then move Riftrite.app somewhere (Applications is fine).
  2. The build isn't signed by a paid Apple developer account, so the first
     time macOS will block it. Right-click (or Control-click) Riftrite.app and
     choose Open, then Open again. On newer macOS, if there's no Open button:
     try to open it once, then go to System Settings > Privacy & Security
     and click "Open Anyway" near the bottom.

PLAYING
  The game is being rebuilt around heroes and an open arena. This build has
  the first whole run: Act 1, seven days, and endless after it as a
  testing option (NEW IN THIS BUILD: see ENDLESS below). Your uploaded art is in the game. The arena
  is turned (your heroes at the bottom, the enemies at the top) and stands
  on the floating island, rocks are ruins, and Rift Collapse eats the edge
  as rift-light. Items, relics, and upgrades have their kind's frame with an
  icon, camp's options and places have theirs, the Pedlar and the Magpie
  stand in their scenes, and the route is the act map. The fonts are Cinzel
  and Alegreya. Practice (the Act 1 fights one at a time, with every path
  free to try) is still on the title.

  A RUN (Title > New run)
  1. Vow each hero to one of their three paths: a small taste of it now,
     with a cost. Fill the path's deed in fights and the hero transforms
     (a new form and signature). Until then you can switch the vow between
     fights (the hero's panel: Switch vow). If two vowed paths are bonded,
     "a bond stirs": it wakes when both heroes have transformed.
  2. Each day:
       Route    the act map: an island a day, each with its two fights (an
                easier one and a harder one that pays more). Days 3 and 5
                are elites; day 7 is Old Mother Ash. Click one of today's
                fights to read it, then Fight this.
       Loadout  put items from the stash into each hero's three slots (any
                hero can hold anything; one tactic each; "no effect" says
                where it does nothing). Then To the fight.
       Fight    place and fight as in Practice; Continue records it.
       After    a win or a tie pays shards and offers an upgrade (one card
                per hero, now and then a wild card), or take 3 shards
                instead. An elite or a Rift Tear win offers a relic, or
                neither. A filled deed transforms the hero.
       Pedlar   after every fight: buy charms, tactics, sigils, and
                gambits, a relic, treat wounds, reroll, or sell an item
                back for half. After the boss he has a legendary.
       Node     then one of the day's nodes, for tomorrow:
                Camp (always): a place with three options; take one: Train
                (an upgrade pick), Hunt (a small fight now for shards;
                losing it isn't a loss), Rest (clears wounds), Scout (see
                the next two days' enemies placed), Map the Rift (swap one
                of tomorrow's fights), Fortify (a small Shield next fight),
                Dig In (a rock in your zone next fight: click a hex while
                placing), Shrine (offer 15 shards or a wound for a rare
                relic, or a relic for one a tier higher; nothing is given
                up unless you take it).
                Rift Tear: pick a depth. Tomorrow's enemies start Shielded;
                Deep adds a rift modifier and Abyssal two (shown on the
                cards); win that fight for relics: 2 rares, 2 epics, or a
                legendary and an epic.
                The Magpie (from day 3, at most twice): charms at rank II
                and a relic, cheap; one look. He buys and swaps relics.
                Event: a scene with two choices, each giving something
                (some at a cost: a wound, a lost signature next fight);
                you can always walk away. Sometimes it's a Bloodied Oath:
                two oaths, each on a hero; take one for 2 fights of a
                burden with the hero's deeds counting double, or pass.
                Then On to the next day. Whatever a node set up for
                tomorrow's fight holds if you lose it and replay the day.
  3. A hero who falls gets a wound (less max HP, up to 3); each won fight
     heals one wound on every hero first. A lost fight
     replays the day; the second loss ends the run. Winning against Old
     Mother Ash wins it; after the boss shop, in a testing run, you can
     end the run there or go deeper into endless (see ENDLESS).
  4. The run is saved after everything you do: Title > Continue the run.
     The hero bar along the bottom shows each hero's wounds, deed progress,
     and items; click a card for the hero's panel.

  ENDLESS (after Old Mother Ash and her shop; a testing option)
  New in this build: the game is getting Acts 2 and 3, and real endless
  will come after Act 3. Until then, endless after Act 1 is a testing
  option: tick "Endless after Act 1 (for testing)" on the New run screen
  (it's off by default; without it, the run ends after Act 1's boss shop).
  With it on, after the boss shop: End the run (a win), or Go deeper
  (testing) with the same heroes, items, relics, and shards. Each floor is a day with one fight, then a node (new in
  this build: no Pedlar and no upgrades on floors; the pick after a fight
  offers only apex cards, once a hero reaches an apex). Every floor the rift grows: enemies have
  15% more HP and attack than the floor before (it compounds), Rift
  Collapse starts a second sooner, and crumbled ground burns 15% hotter.
  Every 3rd floor a rift modifier joins for good (they're listed on the
  route). Every 5th floor is an elite, every 10th is Old Mother Ash
  (she offers boss relics again). The Magpie can still turn up. There are no
  replays: the first loss ends the run, and the floor you fell on is your
  score (the end screen keeps your deepest). Big numbers shorten (12.4k).
  Endless isn't tuned yet: we only measured how far a bot gets.

  APEXES (new in this build: all 18)
  The apex vow opens as you leave Act 1's boss shop (new in this build:
  it was on Go deeper), so in a testing run it's open at the choice: a
  hero who has transformed can vow to one of their path's two apexes (its taste comes at once), and when the
  apex deed fills, they transform again. Switch the apex vow for free
  until then (the day screen or the hero's panel). Every path has two
  apexes, six a hero; some carry the team, some guard it, some help the
  carry. Each apex has a snowball (it grows through the fight) and two
  cards of its own, offered in the after-fight pick once it's reached.
  The apex deeds are a stand-in size for now (aimed at floor 3-4; in the
  full game an apex should land after Act 2's boss). In Practice,
  transform a hero and their panel offers Vow apex and Reach apex.

  PRACTICE (Title > Practice)
  1. Title > Practice, then pick a fight ("Place your heroes").
  2. Drag Brannoc, Maren, and Vell onto your side's hexes (the three rows
     at the bottom, tinted gold; the enemies are at the top). Hover an enemy
     to read what it does.
  3. Click a hero's card in the hero bar along the bottom to open their
     panel (new in this build; clicking the hero on the board no longer
     does). In a fight, it pauses and the panel is for reading.
     Back and forward (< >) move between the heroes; Close, Escape, or a
     click beside it shuts it.
       Path     each hero has three paths. Vow one to try its taste (a small
                piece of the path, with a cost), or Transform to try the
                whole thing: a new form, a new signature, and a new cost.
                Base takes the path away. Each card says where the path
                wants the hero to stand, and its deed: what the hero does
                that the path counts (after a fight, the card shows how much
                that fight put into it). In the full game you vow at the
                start and transform when the deed fills; here both are free.
       Kit      what the hero can do now, at their stage.
       Loadout  the hero's tactic (None, Casters first, Hold your ground,
                Wait to heal; see last build's notes). Transformed
                Wardweaver can't take Wait to heal, since her new signature
                doesn't heal.
     The board names each hero's path and tactic under them; a transformed
     hero stands as their new form. Transformed Trapper Maren places two
     snares before the fight: drag the green markers onto your side or the
     middle row.
     The nine paths:
       Maren    Deadeye (plant and shoot from far away), Trapper (snares
                in the enemies' path), Volley (split shots, shoots on the
                move)
       Brannoc  Hearthwall (Guard: takes part of the hits on allies behind
                or near him; a wall that stops shots), Ironbrand (hits the
                enemies around his target; leaps into groups), Last Watch
                (won't fall once; strongest when nearly dead)
       Vell     Lanternbearer (heals spill onto nearby allies; a healing
                lantern), Wardweaver (overhealing becomes Shield; a circle
                that Slows enemies and Wards allies), Vigil Keeper (Mend
                smites; a beam that burns enemies and heals allies)
  4. Fight. It plays by itself:
       Space   pause            1 2 3   speed 0.5x, 1x, 2x
       S       skip to the end  L       open or close the combat log
       T       every target line (for testing)
     "Combo readout (for testing)", beside the target lines, lists each
     hero's engines (how often each fired, how much it added) and adds how
     each number was made to the log's lines. It's for testing, off by
     default; working combos out is meant to be part of the game.
     The damage chart is on the right. Click a unit to see only its lines in
     the log. Pause and click a hero for their details.
  5. At the end: the result names each hero's path and what the fight put
     into each of their deeds. Rematch (same placement, new crits), Watch
     again, or Place again to try another formation. Back goes to the list.

  Nothing is saved: your formation, paths, snares, tactics, and speed
  last until you close the game.

CHANGED AFTER YOUR FIRST RUN
  - Wounds: every won fight now heals one wound on each hero (before
    anyone who fell there takes a new one).
  - Brannoc is tougher: HP 630 (was 420), DEF 50 (was 30).
  - Every fight's strength was retuned so difficulty climbs: basic fights
    are the easiest, then harder ones, then elites, then Old Mother Ash.
  - Last Watch and Hearthwall need a little less to transform.
  Tell us if Brannoc still falls too often, and in which fights.

TUNED IN THIS BUILD (phase 6: a bot that plays well, then Act 1 tuned to it)
  - Act 1 is harder, most of all from day 4 on and Old Mother Ash: enemies
    have up to 60% more HP and attack. A bot that places by reading the
    fight (never trying it first) wins about half its runs; one that
    places at random wins none.
  - The boss shop is after the boss, and the boss pays 60 shards.
  - Volley reaches 4 hexes again; her vow's cost is now that her shots
    deal 5% less.
  - Wardweaver's Mend also gives its target a small Shield, so her deed
    fills every run (and needs more: 120).
  - Hearthwall, Lanternbearer, and Last Watch need less to transform;
    Volley a little less.
  - Second pass: every transformation is a bigger step up. Each lifts
    HP, ATK, and MGK a little, and what makes each path itself is
    stronger (Heartseeker, Bramble Field, the split shot and Arrow Storm,
    the Hearthbrand Mace and Brand Slam, Last Stand and Grief, Warding
    Circle and Weave, Sunfall and Mend). Act 1's enemies rose to match,
    most from day 4 on and Old Mother Ash.
  - Hearthwall's vowed Guard covers every ally within 2 hexes (not only
    one behind him), so his deed fills in most fights; Wide Guard now
    makes Guard reach 3 hexes.
  Tell us: does Act 1 feel hard but fair? Which fight ends your runs?
  Does transforming feel like a real step up?

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW ABOUT APEXES
  Does reaching an apex feel like the team's peak, a big step past the
  transformation? Which apexes feel like a carry, and which feel weak?
  Does the right team of three apexes feel much stronger than a mixed
  one? Is the apex vow clear on the day screen and in the panel? Does
  the apex land at a good time (for now: around floor 3-4)?

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW ABOUT ENDLESS
  How deep do you get, and what ends your run? Does going deeper feel
  worth it, or does it turn into a wall? Is one fight a floor right? Do
  the gathered rift modifiers feel like new problems, or only harder ones?
  Some choices were made while you were away; tell us if they feel wrong:
    - The Magpie's two visits count afresh when you go deeper and after
      each boss floor.
    - Camp offers no Hunt on a floor; Map the Rift swaps within the
      floor's fights.

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW ABOUT THE ART
  Does the arena read well with heroes at the bottom, on the island? Are
  the item icons clear (each kind's frame says charm, tactic, sigil, graft,
  or relic; the glyph inside is shared until more are drawn)? Does the act
  map make the route clearer? Grafts' rose octagon frame is a stand-in.
  The icons each item got are in docs/plans/rebuild-phase5b-art.md.

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW (playtest gate 3: a whole run)
  Can you play Act 1 start to finish? Does each day's choice (camp, the two
  fights, the loadout, placement, the pick) feel like a real decision? Do
  heroes transform at a good pace (the aim: the first around days 3-4, all
  three by the boss)? Are shards, items, wounds, and relics clear and worth
  thinking about? Do the elites and Old Mother Ash each ask something new?
  What was confusing on the screens?
  Some choices were made while you were away; tell us if they feel wrong:
    - Deed thresholds, pay, and prices are first guesses from the run bot.
    - A hero holds one tactic; the Magpie sells grafts and other gear; a
      wound is treated only at a shop.
    - Rest only clears wounds; a lost fight's wounds and deeds still count.
    - Bonds and a few relics are first versions within what the sim can
      already do (their cards say exactly what they give).

WHAT WE WANTED TO KNOW LAST TIME (playtest gate 2: paths)
  Does each path change where you place the hero and how the fight plays?
  Does a vow feel like a taste of its path, and is its cost felt? Does
  each transformation feel like a different, stronger hero? Can you tell,
  from the board and the log, what each new thing did (snares, Guard, the
  wall, zones, Warded)? Is the hero panel clear?
  Some choices were made while you were away; tell us if they feel wrong:
    - Hearthwall's Guard covers an ally behind Brannoc within 3 hexes
      (vowed), and every ally within 2 hexes (transformed): 1 hex was
      rarely anyone.
    - Transformed Lanternbearer and Wardweaver keep a smaller Mend (or
      Weave) every 4th Lantern Glow, beside their new signature.
    - Deadeye's deed counts damage from beyond 4 hexes, and a unit that
      reaches farther once planted stops walking to plant.
    - Last Watch's deed is damage he deals while he can't fall: base
      Brannoc filled "damage below 30% HP" as much as the vow did.
    - Vow costs that cut damage are small (2%): in these fights even 5%
      of Maren's damage lost many close fights. Is the cost still felt?
    - Guard's share of a hit meets Brannoc's own DEF.
