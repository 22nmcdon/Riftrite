# Act 3: the Shattered Crown (a draft)

Status: **a draft, marked up by the playtester (2026-10-05; Decisions below, all questions answered). Built in phase 8 part 3 (`rebuild-phase8-act3.md`: its pieces in 8c-5a to 8c-5d, its enemies in 8c-6a, its fights in 8c-6b); tuning is 8c-6c.** Names and numbers are placeholders. It follows `rebuild-phase8-acts.md` (section 2a and Decisions 1–18):
- 7 days, with islands as the board rule;
- new faces for about half the archetypes, and Act 1's enemies back in specialized form for the rest;
- the Mimic and the Warden-breaker as new archetypes;
- 2 elites carrying upgrades;
- a final boss that is the rift that has learned you, with the rift learns on for its adds.

Act 2's draft (`act2-glassmere.md`) set the shape this one follows. Questions are in section 10.

## 1. The act at a glance

- **Its question:** can you handle an enemy that answers you?
- **The place:** the rift's heart. Broken islands of floating stone over nothing, joined by narrow bridges, under a crown of shattered light.
- **The days:** as Act 1's and Act 2's: normal, normal, elite, normal, elite, normal, boss.
- **The difficulty** (Decision 3): a team with only its first transformations wins almost never; a team with its apexes wins about half its fights.
- **Enemy growth** (Decision 4): most of a fight's enemies carry a specialization, from day 1 (question CB); elites carry 1–2 upgrades (question CC); the boss's adds are picked by the rift learns (Decision 10).
- **Crumbled ground:** base 25, growth 30, accel 6 (Decision 14).
- **After the boss:** the endless choice, its floors drawn from this act's fights.

The new faces go to the four archetypes that came back in Act 2 (Swarm, Charger, Disruptor, Support). The four Act 1 enemies that sat Act 2 out return here, specialized. That way each archetype gets one new face across the two later acts, which settles Act 2's open question BT (question CA).

## 2. Islands (the board rule)

- **What islands are:** each encounter lists its **void** hexes, the way it lists rocks and water. The rest of the board is two or three islands joined by **bridges**: walkable hexes one hex wide (Decision 6). Nothing can stand on or walk across the void.
- **What crosses the void:**
  - Fliers (the Cinder Moth) cross it.
  - Leaps cross it (Brand Slam, the Rift Hound's Pounce).
  - Shots, areas, pulls, and knockbacks cross it.
  - Walkers can't cross it; they path over the bridges.
- **Falling:** a unit whose center ends a push, pull, or carry over the void falls and is gone, enemies too. The log names the fall and who caused it (rule 4).
  - A hero who falls counts as fallen: a wound, and out of the fight (Decision 17).
  - Leaps, hops, and summons always land on solid ground, as they already pick safe spots.
  - Questions BY and BZ cover the rest.
- **Placement:** your three rows may span more than one island, so where you place decides which island the fight happens on. You can't place heroes on the void.
- **What it asks:** where the edges are. A hero with the void behind them can be pushed off. A bridge is a choke point both ways. An enemy on its own island is reached by leaping, shooting, or walking the bridge.
- **What it does to the paths:**
  - **Ironbrand** comes into its own. Brand Slam leaps across the void onto another island, and its knockback can push an enemy off an edge, which kills it outright.
  - **Hearthwall** holds a bridge. The Unbroken Gate's wall across a bridge closes it to walkers until it breaks.
  - **Trapper's** snares on a bridgehead catch everything that crosses, and snares that snag stop a Cragram's charge.
  - **Deadeye and Vigil Keeper** shoot across the void from an island nothing can walk to quickly.
  - **Volley** keeps moving within her island. Windrunner's hops land safe.
  - **Last Watch** holds a bridge alone.
  - **Lanternbearer's** lantern lights one island. A hero hooked across the void leaves its reach.
  - **Wardweaver's** circle Slows enemies on a bridge. Shields don't stop a fall.
  - **The unpushable charm** (built) is a real answer here, as is anything that keeps the back line off the edges.
- **Readability:** the board draws the void as open sky between the islands, and bridges as narrow spans. A unit near an edge shows nothing special, so the player reads the edges from the board. A fall gets its own log line and a board form (falling, gone).

## 3. The new faces

Each tests one positioning question, with answers in the paths, and each has two specializations.

### Cliffmite (Swarm)

*Threat line:* "Swarms you, and shoves you toward the edge."

- **What it does:** small, quick, and many. When 3 or more Cliffmites are attacking the same hero, each bite also shoves that hero half a hex toward the nearest edge.
- **It tests:** not fighting a swarm with the void at your back; clearing them fast.
- **Answers:** standing in an island's middle; Volley, Hailstorm, and Forgebreaker clearing them; Hearthwall's Guard; the unpushable charm.
- **Specializations:**
  - **Pack Mite:** 2 Cliffmites on a hero are enough to shove.
  - **Brittle Mite:** when it falls, it bursts and Slows heroes within 1 hex by 30% for 2s.

### Cragram (Charger)

*Threat line:* "Charges, and keeps you going."

- **What it does:** charges the farthest hero in reach, like the Cairn Guardian, and knocks the first hero it hits 2 hexes back along its line.
- **It tests:** never standing in its line with the void behind you; spacing on bridges.
- **Answers:**
  - standing so a charge pushes you toward the island, not off it;
  - The Unbroken Gate's wall in its path;
  - Trapper's snares that snag charges;
  - the unpushable charm;
  - killing it from range before it charges.
- **Specializations:**
  - **Thundering Cragram:** the knockback carries every hero in the line, not just the first.
  - **Stunning Cragram:** the hero it hits is Stunned for 1s after landing.

### Gulf Angler (Disruptor)

*Threat line:* "Hooks you across the gap, alone."

- **What it does:** stands on an island apart from yours and hooks the hero farthest from their allies across the void, landing them beside it. A hook always carries its catch all the way to solid ground (question BZ), so it isolates rather than kills.
- **It tests:** the back line's spacing; who stands alone; what your team does with a hero stranded on the wrong island.
- **Answers:**
  - Deadeye or Vigil Keeper killing it across the void first;
  - keeping the back line close together;
  - Ironbrand leaping after the hooked hero;
  - the unpushable charm, which a hook can't move.
- **Specializations:**
  - **Reeling Angler:** a hooked hero is Rooted for 1s on landing.
  - **Twin-Hook Angler:** it hooks two heroes at once.

### Spire Chanter (Support)

*Threat line:* "Its island fights harder while it sings."

- **What it does:** while it stands, every enemy on its island takes 25% less damage. It stays back on its island and keeps out of reach.
- **It tests:** bringing the fight to its island, or breaking it from across the void.
- **Answers:** Brand Slam leaping onto its island; Deadeye's Marked shot; knocking it off an edge; Casters first (supports already count as casters); fighting the enemies that cross to you, off its island.
- **Specializations:**
  - **Quickening Chanter:** enemies on its island also attack 20% faster.
  - **Last-Note Chanter:** when it falls, every enemy on its island gains a Shield of 15% of their max HP.

## 5. The new archetypes

### Mirrorwight (Mimic)

*Threat line:* "Casts back the first thing you show it."

- **What it does:** it watches for the first hero signature cast anywhere on the board (Decision 8). If that signature can be copied (Decision 18: built from areas, damage, heals, Shields, and statuses), it keeps a copy for the fight. From then on, its own mana bar casts the copy with its own stats. Heals and Shields go to its allies, and damage and harmful statuses go to your heroes. Until it has a copy, it casts a dull echo, a weak bolt.
- **What can be copied:**
  - Marking Shot, Hold the Line, Mend in each of its forms, Heartseeker, Arrow Storm, Last Rites, Night Lantern, Warding Circle, and Sunfall.
  - Not Bramble Field (snares), Hearthwall (a wall), or Brand Slam (a leap).
  - The board names what it copied, and the log reads "Mirrorwight · Sunfall (copied from Vell)".
- **It tests:** who casts first near it, and what you let it see.
- **Answers:**
  - killing it before it copies (Deadeye, Brand Slam, Casters first);
  - holding a signature back (Wait to heal);
  - letting it copy something that serves it poorly;
  - choosing which signature the team casts first.
- **Specializations:**
  - **Greedy Mirrorwight:** it swaps its copy for each new signature it sees.
  - **Twinned Mirrorwight:** it casts its copy twice, each at 60%.

### Unbinder (Warden-breaker)

*Threat line:* "Breaks your Shields and frees its allies."

- **What it does:**
  - Its attacks deal double damage to Shields.
  - Every 6s it cleanses Roots, Marks, Burn, Poison, Slow, and Stun from its allies within 2 hexes.
  - It answers the keyword builds: Trapper's roots, Deadeye's Marks, Burn relics, and Wardweaver's and Lanternbearer's Shields.
- **It tests:** what your team does when its engine is blunted.
- **Answers:**
  - killing it first;
  - plain damage that needs no keyword (Vigil Keeper, Ironbrand, Forgebreaker);
  - catching enemies beyond its 2 hexes;
  - snares placed where it isn't.
- **Specializations:**
  - **Hungering Unbinder:** each Shield it breaks heals it 10% of its max HP.
  - **Wide Unbinder:** its cleanse reaches every ally on its island.

## 6. The elites

Each is built around one mechanic, says what it does, is previewed days ahead, and carries 1–2 upgrades drawn from `enemy-growth.md` section 3, all shown on the fight card (question CC).

### The Cragherd, "Stampede"

- **Who:** a Great Cragram (the leader), two Cragrams, and a Spire Chanter, across two islands joined by two bridges.
- **The mechanic:** every 15s the whole herd charges at once. The Great Cragram knocks its hero 3 hexes back.
- **What answers it:**
  - standing in the middle of your island between stampedes, never with the void behind you in a charge's line;
  - walls and snares on the bridges;
  - the unpushable charm;
  - killing the Chanter first, or fighting the herd off its island.

### The Mirror Court, "Every Answer Echoed"

- **Who:** the Mirror Queen (the leader), two Mirrorwights, and a Rift-Worn Sentinel holding the bridge to their island.
- **The mechanic:** the Queen copies every copyable signature she sees, not only the first. Each time she copies one, her court gets the copy too, cast at her full strength.
- **What answers it:**
  - showing them few signatures early;
  - killing the Queen first (Deadeye, Casters first) or leaping to her;
  - Wait to heal;
  - area damage on a court that stands together.

## 7. The boss: the Heart of the Rift

The rift itself: the knot of shattered light at the crown of the islands. It doesn't copy your tricks; it has learned your habits and answers them (Decision 8). It's built to be fought by a team with its apexes.

| When | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Start** | Three islands: yours at the bottom, the Heart's at the top beyond the void (one bridge in the middle), and a shelf on each side joined to both by narrow bridges. The Heart doesn't move. It's **Warded** (it takes half damage) while its **Learned Host** stands: four adds from Act 3's roster, whose specializations and upgrades the rift learns picks to blunt your top 1–2 habits (Decision 10; all shown on the fight card). |
| **Below 70%: Severing** | Every 12s a bridge is warned (3s), then breaks; it reforms 10s later. A unit on it when it breaks falls. Two more learned adds join. |
| **Below 35%: Unmaking** | The Ward is gone. Every 8s the Heart pushes heroes within 2 hexes of it 1 hex away; its island is wide enough that the push never reaches an edge, so it breaks up a melee crowd without dropping anyone (Decision 6). Rift Collapse starts. A race. |

- **The rift learns** (`enemy-growth.md` section 5, normal difficulty, the boss's adds only):
  - It reads the run's last 3 fights (keywords applied, healing, Shields, Stealth, mana spent, where the heroes stood) and counters your top 1–2 habits.
  - It swaps up to half of the adds' specializations and upgrades for ones from section 5's table. A team leaning on Roots meets Anchored adds; a bunched team meets Drifting Moths.
  - It never makes an enemy immune, and everything it picked is on the fight card.
- **What it tests:** kill order against adds built to counter you; reading the bridges as they break; then a race as the rift closes in.
- **What answers it:**
  - **Start:** area damage for the host (Forgebreaker, Hailstorm); Eagle Eye and Stormline across the void to the Heart once its Ward is down; a second plan for whichever habit it counters.
  - **Severing:** staying off a warned bridge; a leap (Brand Slam) doesn't need the bridges at all.
  - **Unmaking:** burst and sustain (The Beacon, Undying Oath); ranged damage from outside the push; the unpushable charm for a melee hero who stays close.

## 9. What it needs in the sim

Each piece is skipped by a fight that doesn't use it, so Acts 1 and 2's fights and the bench's fingerprints stay as they are.

1. **Void hexes** (`"void"` on an encounter, like water):
   - the route can't cross them, though nothing else is blocked (shots and areas fly over);
   - fliers ignore them, and placement can't use them;
   - spots picked to land on (leaps, hops, summons) stay off them, as they already stay off crumbled ground;
   - the board draws them.
2. **Falling:** a unit whose push, pull, or carry ends with its center over the void falls. It's removed, with a new log kind FELL naming who caused it (an audit rule and a board form). It counts as a fall for a hero (Decision 17) and as a kill for whoever caused it (question BY).
3. **Islands:** which island a unit stands on (the walkable hexes joined together), for the Spire Chanter's aura, the Wide Unbinder, and the Last-Note Chanter: a `UnitCondition` "on its island".
4. **Bridges that break and reform:** a new effect, like `flood` but for the void, with a log kind of its own (or WATER's shape reused). No existing effect changes the ground this way, so it's new code. The Heart of the Rift needs it.
5. **The Mirrorwight's copy:** the first copyable hero signature cast anywhere on the board, read from the log. It's kept as the Mirrorwight's own signature, with its sides turned: heals and Shields to its allies, damage and harmful statuses to the heroes. It needs new code, since nothing copies an ability today.
6. **The Unbinder:** double damage to Shields (shared with the Shieldbreaker upgrade), and a cleanse on a timer (cleanse with `statuses` is built).
7. **Pushes from swarms and stampedes:** the Cliffmite's shove when 3 or more bite one hero (a count of attackers on a target), and the Cragherd's charging all at once (built pieces on a timer).
8. **Upgrades** (`enemy-growth.md` section 3, the 11): kit mods on elites and the boss.
   - Most are built pieces: Frenzied, Warded, Swift, Thick-Hided, Vengeful, and Anchored's unpushable.
   - Some are new: Watchful (targets stealthed heroes), Cinder-Skinned (Burn on it halved), Shieldbreaker, Mark-Shy, Rift-Touched, and Anchored's cap on Roots.
   - They're drawn fresh for each elite and shown on the card.
9. **The rift learns:** a summary of the last 3 fights in run state, and the swap for the boss's adds (a run piece, not a sim one).
10. **The Dazzling Moth's misses:** heroes inside Ember Dust miss 30% of their attacks. A miss chance from a zone is new; the built dodge is on a timer.
11. **The rest is built:** Act 3's crumbled ground (Decision 14), `act3.json` with endless after it, a higher share of specialized enemies, and the good bot reading edges and the void (new placement features, like water's).

## Decisions

The playtester, 2026-10-05:

1. **A pushed enemy that falls is a kill for whoever pushed it** (Question BY): on-kill triggers and kill deeds count it.
2. **A unit falls only when its center ends a push, pull, or carry over the void** (Question BZ). An enemy's hook always carries its catch to solid ground, so hooks isolate rather than kill. Knockbacks and charges can drop a hero.
3. **The four Act 1 enemies that sat Act 2 out return in Act 3, specialized** (Question CA, left to the draft; answers Act 2's BT). Act 2's faces and regulars sit Act 3 out.
4. **Three in four of a fight's enemies are specialized, rounded down, from day 1** (Question CB).
5. **Elites carry 1–2 upgrades, drawn fresh and shown on the card; the boss and normal enemies carry none** (Question CC). The boss's adds have the rift learns.
6. **The Unmaking's push is softened** (Question CE: "the push would be hard to get through, maybe impossible"): every 8s it pushes only heroes within 2 hexes of the Heart, 1 hex away, and the Heart's island is wide enough that the push never reaches an edge, so it can't drop a hero.
7. **Casters first counts the Mirrorwight, and supports such as the Spire Chanter** (Question CD: "support is considered caster"). Supports already count; the tactic adds the `mimic` archetype.
8. **The Mirrorwight copies the first copyable signature cast anywhere on the board, for now** (Question CF).
9. **The rift learns swaps only the boss's adds' specializations and upgrades, no rift modifiers, for now** (Question CG).
10. **Rift Collapse on islands is crumbled ground that hurts, as in the other acts, for now** (Question CH): it never turns into void.
11. **The names stand** (Question CI).
